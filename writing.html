What I Learned Building a Data Warehouse From Real-World Data

A working essay about messy operational data, business rules, dimensional modeling, and the difference between calculating a number and representing the business behind it.

The problem wasn't the arithmetic.

It is tempting to think project profitability is simple: take revenue, subtract cost, and calculate the margin. Real operational data makes that assumption much less comfortable.

My first model could calculate profitability. The harder question was whether it represented all of the economics that mattered.

Then the business showed me what the model was missing.

Management labor was not represented in the normal payroll feed because salaried owners and managers were not tracked there in the same way as field staff. Other project costs also lived outside the initial labor and laboratory picture, including fixed-price reports and assessments.

That changed the modeling problem. The goal became not merely to make SQL produce a result, but to design a system that more faithfully represents how a project actually consumes resources.

The work continues.

The warehouse is being refined as the business rules become clearer. That process—testing, finding gaps, revising the model, and validating the result—is part of the project rather than something hidden from it.
